a snapshot of what i'm focused on right now. · last updated june 2026

current status

Building systems that support curiosity.

I'm Kitty — a data analyst, privacy advocate, and chaos engineer based in the midwest. This summer isn't about optimization. It's about paying attention. After finishing my degree, I decided to stop treating every interest like it needed to become a career move — instead I'm spending my time on the things that genuinely fascinate me: health data, privacy, home labs, body mechanics, books, plants, and whatever strange rabbit hole catches my attention next.

What happens when I pay attention long enough to understand how something actually works?

Mornings are slow on purpose — late starts, a protein drink, sunshine, chores, and a Vox Machina episode. Afternoons shift into coding, organizing the book collection, more reading, Pilates, and hanging upside down on the yoga trapeze with sunshine sprinkled in between. Yard work has become my reset after an hour of doomscrolling, and the houseplants are propagating like they know something I don't.

what i'm working on

chronically_quantified.

The main project right now is sorting and cleaning roughly 8 years of Fitbit, diet app, and other health data into something coherent — partly out of genuine curiosity about my own patterns, and partly because it's a great excuse to get better at data pipelines. Old exports, inconsistent formats, and the occasional mystery CSV are all part of the fun.

Alongside that: de-Googling my life one service at a time, poking at Arduino and Raspberry Pi projects for the love of it, and — for entirely petty reasons — dumbing down my smart TVs so they stop phoning home. Guides for all of this will eventually make their way to the portfolio, once they're less "duct tape and curiosity" and more "something another person could follow."

The less glamorous but equally real work right now: sorting accumulated projects and giving them proper READMEs and folder structure. Labeling things for future me — the version who shows up whenever the energy does — is its own kind of skill. Externalizing structure so my brain doesn't have to hold it. It counts.

what i'm learning

Going deeper.

Lately I've been living at the intersection of data, privacy, and systems thinking. Right now that means data engineering fundamentals (mostly via the health data pipeline — eight years of exports do not clean themselves), home lab infrastructure, signal tracing, self-hosting, and just enough electronics to make Arduino and Raspberry Pi projects behave.

The longer I study how information moves through systems, the more I find myself interested in how bodies move through systems too. The more I learn about technology, the more interested I become in biology. The more I learn about biology, the more interested I become in systems design. Turns out they're not that different.

I learn best when I'm building something a little chaotic that I care about. Structured courses alone put me to sleep.

what i'm reading

Currently on my desk.

I read several things at once and I refuse to apologize for it.

Storytelling with Data — Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic

Learning to find the story before reaching for a chart. Working on chart selection and what I'm calling "upstream thinking" — identifying what actually needs to be said before touching the data.

Modern Data — Kelvin Lin

Getting into the infrastructure side of analytics. Understanding how data actually moves through organizations, not just how to analyze it once it lands.

How to Write Clearly — Tom Albrighton

Because good analysis that nobody can understand is just expensive noise. Clarity is a technical skill and I'm treating it like one.

Somatic Yoga for Nervous System Regulation — LearnWell Books

Less "reading" and more "ongoing reference" at this point — it has a guide for specific poses, so it's the kind of book I keep coming back to rather than finishing.

All About Love — bell hooks

Just started this one. Already suspicious it's going to rearrange some furniture in my head.

Caught Up — Nessa Allen

Spicy romance on the physical stack. Sometimes a book just needs to be fun and nobody has to justify that.

A Soul to Touch — Opal Reyne audiobook

Picked this up after finishing Wave Song. Commute-stack monster romance — the read-while-doing-other-things slot remains a sacred slot.

outside the terminal

The rest of it.

A lot of my actual life right now happens away from a screen, on purpose. Pilates and hanging upside down on the yoga trapeze are non-negotiable parts of the day, and the overgrown backyard has become a genuine project — slow, physical, and weirdly meditative. When I notice I've been doomscrolling for an hour, going outside to pull weeds or move dirt around is the reset that actually works. I've become increasingly convinced that touching grass is an underrated debugging technique.

My body has accidentally become one of my favorite research projects too — tracking movement, recovery, and patterns instead of forcing outcomes. Understanding comes first. Optimization can wait.

Indoors, the houseplants are propagating with more enthusiasm than I planned for, Vox Machina is the current morning-coffee show, and the book collection is slowly getting organized into something that resembles a system. Pottery is on hold but not forgotten, and I still have long-horizon plans for biotope aquariums and plant automation, because domestic science is still science and I will not be taking questions.

a few things i currently believe

Subject to revision.

  • Data should serve people, not exploit them.
  • Curiosity is more useful than certainty.
  • Small systems beat big goals.
  • Documentation is an act of kindness to your future self.
  • Most interesting things reveal themselves when you pay attention long enough.
  • Sometimes the correct response to a problem is a spreadsheet. Sometimes it's a nap. Both have their place.
